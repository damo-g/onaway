Ranking · round 2

  1. C · Button to ticket · 9.1. The yellow button you pressed becomes the top of your ticket: it travels and settles as the ticket's header (the yellow fades to the ticket's colour on the way), then the ticket unrolls down from it and its lines fill in. Cause and effect in one object, and the yellow never grows past the button. About 1.5s; the form is usable at 0.6s.
  2. A · Ticket issued · 8.9. Round 1's favourite, taken further and slowed down: the page you leave steps back, the headline wipes in left to right, the ticket rises from below and settles without a bounce, then its lines fill in one by one. The most subtle of the four.
  3. B · Ticket feed · 8.4. The ticket feeds up through a slot in four short steps, like a ticket machine printing it for you, a print line flickering at each step. The most character, a little more time (about 1.6s).
  4. D · Handed over · 7.8. The ticket slides in from the side with a slight tilt, as if passed across a counter. Warm, but sideways motion competes with the left-to-right reading of the page.

When it runs

  • Only for links to /quote/: the nav's yellow button, the Steps tear-off, the quote panel, the service pages' doors and buttons, About's and Help's end bands.
  • C needs a button to start from; from a plain text link it plays A.
  • Leaving /quote/ uses the general transition (PT-03).
  • Reduced motion: a 150ms crossfade.

How it would be built

  • Native View Transitions tagged quote on pageswap.
  • C: the pressed button and the ticket's header share a view-transition-name, so the browser carries one into the other; the unroll is a clip-path on the ticket, the lines a short stagger.
  • A and B: ::view-transition-new on the ticket plus the same line stagger; B's slot is one temporary element.
