PT-01 · Service Arrivals

From Option

Pick where you start (the R2-E cards, the hero's mini cards, or the Services drop) and an option; it plays. Or click any service link inside the frame. Every option lands on the real service hero: breadcrumb, headline, the rail, the hanging band and the two doors.

Ranking · round 2

  1. A · Sign arrives · 9.3. The card you click becomes the service page's hanging band: it travels and reshapes into the band, the rail draws in above it, and the doors hang down and settle. Now it lands on the real page, so the story is complete: you picked a sign, you're standing under it. About 1.6s, but the page is readable at 0.5s.
  2. E · Hang & settle · 8.9. No shared object: the page you leave lifts away, the headline settles, the rail draws left to right and the band hangs down with a soft swing, then the doors. The same from every link (nav, footer, map), so it's the fallback for A wherever there's no card to carry.
  3. D · Rail run · 8.6. The card collapses into a line and the line becomes the page's rail; the sign then hangs from it. Clever and very "route", but the card disappears into a line before the sign comes back, so it reads as two ideas rather than one.
  4. F · Flip sign · 8.0. The card flips edge-on like a board flap and flips back as the band. Crisp and transit-like; the flip is quicker and more mechanical than the brand's caregiver side wants.
  5. B · Route line · 7.2. My take: a good mark, the wrong job. The band crossing the screen is a generic wipe that hides the page and doesn't land anywhere on it. D keeps its idea (a line that takes you somewhere) and makes it the page's own rail, so B can retire.
  6. C · Arrival board · 7.0. Charming once, too long and too covering for a link people use often. Best kept as a one-off (the 404, or launch day).

The hero's mini cards

  • Yes, the same interaction. They are the same three links, so they should arrive the same way. Switch From to Hero cards: the Paper card darkens to Carbon as it travels, then becomes the band. The colour change does the explaining.
  • The Services drop and the footer rows carry it too (From: Services drop): the row becomes the band.
  • Links with no object of their own (Services in the nav, the map's results) use E.

How it would be built

  • A: each card, mini card and drop row gets view-transition-name: sign-cleaning (etc.); the service page's band has the same name. The browser carries one into the other; the rail and doors are a normal entrance animation on the new page (the one it has today, softened).
  • Only the clicked card is named at the moment of leaving (set on pageswap), so the three never compete.
  • Reduced motion: a 150ms crossfade. Firefox: a normal page load.
