PT-02 · Quote Arrivals

Option

Click "Get a free quote" in the nav (or on a service page) inside the frame, or press Play. Other links use a plain crossfade here.

Ranking

  1. A · Tear-off · 9.0. The yellow button grows into a stub with its perforation, fills the screen, then tears away along it, and the quote form is underneath. It finishes the gesture the Steps ticket starts (its yellow "Get a free quote" tear-off), so the site's main action has one consistent signature. The only full-screen yellow on the site, and it lasts a moment. About 1s.
  2. B · Ticket issued · 8.6. The page settles, and the form's ticket slides up from the bottom of the screen, as if issued from a machine, and lands where it lives. Calm and clear; it puts the eye straight on the form. About 0.85s.
  3. C · Punch · 7.2. A conductor's punch: the quote page opens as a circle from where you clicked, and the chad drops. Quick and playful, but a circle reveal is common on the web and says less about tickets than A or B. About 0.6s.

When it runs

  • Only for links to /quote/: the nav's yellow button, the Steps tear-off, the quote panel, the service pages' and About's buttons, the help page's end band.
  • A grows from whichever button was pressed. From a link with no button shape it grows from the click point.
  • Leaving /quote/ uses the general transition (PT-03).
  • Reduced motion: a 150ms crossfade.

How it would be built

  • The same native View Transitions as PT-01, tagged quote on pageswap.
  • A: the button carries a view-transition-name that matches a full-screen yellow layer on the quote page, which the browser grows into; the tear is a short CSS animation on that layer. The yellow layer must never be visible without the transition (it's removed on pagereveal).
  • B: the form's ticket animates in with ::view-transition-new. The simplest of the three.
  • C: a clip-path circle from the click point, passed across with sessionStorage.
