Round 2, from your notes

  • Receipt keeps D’s layout and hierarchy but takes the site’s ticket: the face and stub tints from TS-02, the Stamp · subtle edge, dashed rows like the quote ticket, values in Instrument Sans, labels in mono, and the logo (W symbol + wordmark) at the top. The stub tears off with the barcode and the ref.
  • Board ticket is the arrival board as a Carbon ticket with paper perforations. The ref is in a yellow dot matrix (or split-flap), and the sub-headings (Job, When, Contact, Notes, Sent from) are Instrument Sans 20.
  • Boarding pass picks up the logo strip, the perforated edge and the same sub-headings.
  • Paper stub is dropped. Kraft (camel with a fibre grain, as in FT-02) works as a whole surface or just the stub.

How it holds up in inboxes

  • The perforation and grain are small background images. Gmail, Apple Mail, iOS and Outlook.com show them. Outlook desktop shows a clean straight edge, which still reads fine.
  • The dot matrix is built from table cells, not an image, so it shows even with images off. With images off, the logo falls back to the word "ONAWAY".
  • Gmail strips web fonts and uses the system sans and mono. Apple Mail can load Instrument Sans and Overpass Mono from onaway.ca.
  • Every email stays under Gmail’s 102 KB clipping limit, and the plain-text version still goes with it.

Ranking

  1. Receipt · Stamp · subtle · Paper · 9.2. Your favourite layout, now unmistakably ONAWAY. It reads like the ticket on the quote page and keeps a long team email scannable.
  2. Board ticket · Dot matrix · Kraft stub · 8.8. The strongest brand moment. A heavy dark block for a customer confirmation, but excellent for the team’s inbox.
  3. Boarding pass · Stamp · subtle · 8.3. Clear, with a big route. The header strip and the grid make it the busiest of the three.

A hybrid worth considering: Receipt for the customer and Board ticket for the team. Each audience gets the version that suits it, and both share the same edge and stub.
