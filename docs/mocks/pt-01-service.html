PT-01 · Service Arrivals

Option

Click a service card (or Services in the nav) inside the frame, or press Play. Other links use a plain crossfade here; they have their own mocks (PT-02 quote, PT-03 general).

Ranking

  1. A · Sign arrives · 9.2. The card you click becomes the service page's sign: it shrinks into the hero tile while the page settles in around it. Nothing new is invented; it's the same object continuing, which reads as "you are now at this stop". About 0.7s.
  2. B · Route line · 8.3. A Carbon band carrying the pictogram runs across like a train passing, and the service page is there behind it. Strong and on-brand, but every service visit runs the full width, so it gets heavier on the third visit than A. About 0.75s.
  3. C · Arrival board · 7.4. Board slats flap down, read NOW ARRIVING · MOVING in dot matrix, then flap away. The most "transit", but it holds the visitor for over a second and hides both pages at once. Better saved for a one-off moment than a link people use often. About 1.3s.

When it runs

  • Any link to /cleaning/, /moving/ or /delivery/: the R2-E cards, the hero service cards, the Services drop, the footer's Get around rows, and the map's results.
  • A and B use the clicked service's pictogram. From a link without one (the nav), A falls back to its page settle without the moving sign.
  • The navbar never moves.
  • Reduced motion: a 150ms crossfade for every option.

How it would be built

  • Native cross-document View Transitions (CSS @view-transition { navigation: auto }), no framework. A few lines of JS on pageswap tag the navigation as service from the link's address; the CSS picks the animation with :active-view-transition-type(service).
  • A: the card's tile and the service hero's tile share a view-transition-name; the browser morphs one into the other. The cheapest of the three to build.
  • B: a clip-path wipe on the new page plus one band element. C needs its own temporary board element, so it's the most work.
  • Chrome, Edge and Safari 18.2+ animate; Firefox simply navigates as today.
