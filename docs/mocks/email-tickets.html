Rendered from the real email HTML · sample request: Moving, Mississauga → Oakville, Wed Nov 4

A · Boarding pass

A paper ticket on a darker paper page. Carbon header strip, the route in big caps, every detail in a two-column grid, then a notched tear and a stub with the ref and the quote time.

The most "ticket" of the four, and the team version scans well because of the grid. In Outlook desktop the notches come out square, which still reads as a ticket.

B · Arrival board

A Carbon departures board: the ref in split-flap tiles, mono rows, one yellow status chip ("Due 4:15 PM" / "Quote in writing by").

The strongest brand moment and the fastest to read on a phone. It's dark, so Gmail's dark mode leaves it almost untouched; the paper words above it may be inverted.

C · Paper stub

The quietest: today's email with a stub card added: a dotted perforation down the left edge and a punched hole. The team version stacks label over value in airy groups.

Closest to what's live, the safest in every client, and the easiest to keep tidy.

D · Receipt roll

A narrow till receipt: mono throughout, dotted leaders, a barcode drawn from the ref, a torn foot.

The most playful. The barcode is decorative and doesn't scan. All-mono text is slower to read for long notes.

What's the same in all four

  • The customer's words are unchanged: "Got it, {First}. Your request {REF} is in. You’ll have your quote in writing by {time}. Rather talk? Call {phone}." The ticket adds the service, route and day they chose, with no new sentences.
  • The team email keeps every field, including the new Sent from group (device, IP, approx. location) and times with their zone.
  • Email-safe only: tables, inline styles, system fonts (Gmail strips web fonts), no images, no SVG. Labels use the system mono font in place of Overpass Mono.
  • A plain-text version is still sent with every email.
